Teaching

My goal as a professor is to make my students more informed citizens, thinkers, and researchers while helping them practice broadly applicable skills like strategic thinking and clear exposition. Despite the ever-increasing trend in education toward incorporating more technology and AI in the classroom, my teaching philosophy focuses on creating memorable and meaningful social interactions and supporting students through both their academic and personal challenges.

I have taught Theories of International Relations, an upper-level undergraduate course, on two occasions. This syllabus for this class can be accessed below. I am prepared to teach courses broadly in International Relations, Comparative Politics, and Quantitative Methodology, including more specialized courses on civil war, state development, ethnic politics, or international intervention. My regional specialties include the Middle East, Africa, and Southeast Asia.

I also support my students beyond the classroom. Having benefitted tremendously from my experience with Fulbright and the Critical Language Scholarship, I encourage my best students to apply for these prestigious awards. I have served on Fulbright application review committees at both Texas A&M University and the University of Louisville, contributing to multiple successful applications.
